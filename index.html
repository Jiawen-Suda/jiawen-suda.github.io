    International Journal of Biological Macromolecules, 310, 143076 (2025).

    DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2025.143076 ↗

    The COVID-19 pandemic has posed major challenges to global public health, with viral evolution and drug resistance underscoring the need for continued optimization of antiviral therapies. This study develops a computational framework integrating molecular dynamics simulations, free energy perturbation, AlphaFold 3, and deep learning models to systematically investigate the molecular interactions and binding thermodynamics of representative SARS-CoV-2 main protease mutants with nirmatrelvir and substrates, providing a theoretical basis for understanding the molecular mechanisms of resistance mutations and designing next-generation inhibitors.

  3. 2025

    The interface hydrophilic–hydrophobic integration of fluorinated defective graphene towards biomedical applications

    Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics, 27, 7538–7555 (2025).

    DOI: 10.1039/D5CP00075K ↗

    Two-dimensional materials hold considerable potential for biomolecular adsorption and functional regulation. This study combines defect engineering with fluorination to construct fluorinated defective graphene, enabling efficient adsorption and conformational modulation of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease and receptor-binding domain, with the latter undergoing substantial structural collapse on the material surface. Based on these findings, we propose a hydrophilic–hydrophobic integration strategy that harnesses the intrinsic physicochemical properties of materials and amino acids to guide the design of materials for biological interfaces.

  4. 2024

    Patterned graphene: An effective platform for adsorption, immobilization, and destruction of SARS-CoV-2 Mpro

    Journal of Colloid and Interface Science, 673, 202–215 (2024).

    DOI: 10.1016/j.jcis.2024.06.072 ↗

    Patterning enables fine control over the surface structure and chemical properties of materials, providing a route to complex functions and precise applications. Inspired by this approach, this study designs checkerboard and striped graphene and uses molecular dynamics simulations to investigate their adsorption, immobilization, and conformational modulation of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease. Checkerboard patterns favor disruption of the active pocket, whereas striped patterns confine the protein within non-oxidized regions and strengthen binding. These findings reveal how surface patterns differentially regulate protein adsorption and structural responses, offering insights into the precise design of materials for diverse biomedical needs.

  5. 2022

    Bulk structure of Si₂BN predicted by computational approaches

    Diamond and Related Materials, 130, 109530 (2022).

    DOI: 10.1016/j.diamond.2022.109530 ↗

    The rich structural phase space of crystalline materials offers broad opportunities for materials discovery and property design. By combining particle swarm optimization (PSO) with first-principles calculations, this study predicts and identifies α-Si₂BN as a candidate bulk structure, revealing its narrow-band-gap semiconducting character and potential for high-temperature applications. Integrating crystal structure searches with assessments of multiple properties, this work provides a theoretical reference for exploring unknown crystalline phases from chemical compositions and designing functional materials for application-specific needs.

  6. 2022

    The Inhibition of SARS-CoV-2 3CL Mpro by Graphene and Its Derivatives from Molecular Dynamics Simulations

    ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (2022).

    DOI: 10.1021/acsami.1c18104 ↗

    At the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, we began exploring two-dimensional materials for antiviral applications, contributing to early efforts to modulate key SARS-CoV-2 proteins using these materials. Through molecular dynamics simulations, this study compares the adsorption and conformational modulation of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease on pristine graphene, defective graphene, and graphene oxide. Defective graphene markedly disrupts the active-site pocket, suggesting potential inhibitory effects. By linking material structure to protein responses at the molecular level, this work provides a theoretical basis for designing antiviral interfaces and establishes a model system for our subsequent rational material design through defect engineering, fluorination, and patterning.

  7. 2022

    Turning the structure of the Aβ₄₂ peptide by different functionalized carbon nanotubes: a molecular dynamics simulation study

    Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics, 24, 4270–4279 (2022).

    DOI: 10.1039/D1CP04278E ↗

    Abnormal protein conformational changes and aggregation are central questions in protein conformational diseases, and nanomaterials offer new possibilities for modulating these processes. As our first work in molecular dynamics, this study uses molecular dynamics simulations to investigate how differently functionalized carbon nanotubes affect the adsorption and structural evolution of the Aβ₄₂ peptide. It reveals how surface functionalization promotes peptide unfolding and conformational rearrangement by altering adsorption patterns, hydrogen-bond networks, and interactions involving key residues. These findings highlight the potential of functionalized carbon nanotubes to interfere with abnormal Aβ₄₂ aggregation and provide a theoretical basis for controlling protein conformation through material surface chemistry. We have since built on this work to further develop our research in this area.

  8. 2022

    Adsorption of metal atoms on two-dimensional BC₃ and AlC₃ nanosheets: Computational studies

    Chemical Physics Letters, 792, 139403 (2022).

    DOI: 10.1016/j.cplett.2022.139403 ↗

    Combining two-dimensional materials with metal atoms offers a broad design space for tuning material properties and exploring energy and electronic applications. As our first work in density functional theory (DFT), this study uses DFT calculations to systematically examine the adsorption of 30 metal species on BC₃ and AlC₃ nanosheets, comparing preferred adsorption sites, binding strengths, and changes in electronic structure and magnetism. The results show that incorporating boron and aluminum enhances metal-atom adsorption, while adsorbed metals impart diverse electronic and magnetic properties to the substrates. By connecting metal identity and substrate composition with material properties, this work provides a theoretical reference for screening and designing two-dimensional functional materials for energy storage, catalysis, and spintronics. We have since built on this work to further develop our research in this area.

04 / Research philosophy

Research Philosophy & Evolution

From material design to multiscale mechanistic understanding and AI-driven scientific exploration.

My research approach has evolved from searching for and designing materials, to integrating computational methods to understand complex reaction mechanisms, and to exploring the interplay between artificial intelligence and physicochemical models. These changes reflect both methodological advances and the demands of the scientific questions themselves. A central theme is understanding how microscopic interactions determine macroscopic function, and translating that understanding into rational design.

Stage 1 | From material screening to rational design (2019–2023)

During this stage, I primarily used molecular dynamics simulations and density functional theory to study biofunctional materials and materials for energy catalysis, developing an approach that connects structure, interactions, and function.

In biomaterials, we began by investigating how nanomaterials modulate protein conformation and extended this work to key SARS-CoV-2 proteins. Our approach evolved from comparing existing graphene materials to rational design based on the intrinsic physicochemical properties of materials and proteins. Subsequent studies explored defect engineering, patterning, and fluorination, culminating in the concept of hydrophilic–hydrophobic integration in material design.

In energy materials, we used first-principles calculations to investigate interactions between metal atoms and two-dimensional substrates, and their effects on electronic structure, magnetism, and catalytic properties. These studies provided a theoretical basis for screening functional materials and designing electrocatalytic active sites.

This work and its later extensions shifted my focus from finding materials with desired properties to understanding the origins of those properties and using that knowledge to guide design.

Stage 2 | Integrating methods to understand multiscale reaction mechanisms (2024–2026)

As my research expanded from material interfaces to complex biomolecular and chemical reaction systems, it became clear that a single method often cannot capture conformational changes, binding thermodynamics, and bond formation and cleavage together. This stage therefore focuses on integrating molecular simulation, quantum chemistry, and artificial intelligence around specific scientific questions.

In SARS-CoV-2 research, we shifted our attention to main protease mutations and drug resistance. We combined molecular dynamics, free energy perturbation, AlphaFold 3, and deep learning to investigate how representative mutations affect drug and substrate recognition, molecular interactions, and binding thermodynamics.

We also expanded into enzymatic plastic degradation and resource recovery. Motivated by green biomanufacturing, our studies connect the pre-reaction stage of enzyme–substrate interactions with hydrolysis itself. Molecular dynamics, enhanced sampling, and deep learning were combined to understand enhanced hydrolase activity before reaction. QM/MM and the pyCPET model were then used to investigate hydrolysis pathways and temperature dependence, including double-proton shuttling and mechanisms of activity changes and inactivation.

Together, these studies connect pre-reaction conformational behavior with chemical transformations and establish a foundation in multiscale simulation for complex enzyme catalysis.

Stage 3 | Bringing physical mechanisms and artificial intelligence together (2026–)

Progress in computational chemistry depends both on computational advances and on formulating meaningful chemical questions. In the AI for Science (AI4S) era, I aim to explore how artificial intelligence and physicochemical models can work together: using AI to identify patterns in complex data, multiscale simulation to uncover their physical basis, and both to propose and test new designs.

My future work will focus on enzyme catalysis and functional materials, connecting data analysis, mechanistic understanding, property prediction, and design validation. The aim is to move from explaining existing observations toward predicting and rationally designing new systems, functions, and reaction pathways.

Three questions will guide this effort: whether models have a clear physical basis, whether predictions can be tested in new systems, and whether the resulting insights can be translated into verifiable design principles.
